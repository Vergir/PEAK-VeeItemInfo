# What the overlay says, and why

The product decisions: what a line means, where it goes, what is left out on purpose, and
what the settings are for. The game facts behind the numbers are in
[internals_game.md](internals_game.md); the code that produces them is in
[internals_infra.md](internals_infra.md). [showcase.html](showcase.html) is every item's
overlay as it draws in game, generated by the mod from the same code - the accurate picture,
and the baseline this document is written against, but not a specification: it cannot
disagree with the code, so it cannot catch a bug.

---

## No translation, ever

The overlay uses numbers, signs and the game's own icons. There is no English on any rendered
line, and there is to be none: a fact with no symbol is **omitted**, never described in words.
The last prose to go was `GAIN/REMOVE {n} COLD OVER {n}s` on the Heat Pack; before it,
`AFTER 10s,` on poison, the `NEARBY PLAYERS WILL RECEIVE:` header, the biome name on
sunscreen, and the `{n} USES` label.

Three consequences follow. A status icon that could not be scraped falls back to the status
name in capitals, which is a failure mode and not a style; anything that assembles a list of
statuses skips those with no icon rather than print the word. An item's name is never printed:
where a line needs to show an item whose icon could not be packed, it shows the generic item
glyph. And a passive or a behaviour with no agreed symbol stays unsaid, because inventing one
per item is how the overlay turns back into prose.

---

## Four sections

In reading order, each hideable from config, separated by a blank line that can be switched
off:

- **Custom** - facts that are not the result of using the item: how far a rope reaches, how
  long a stovetop burns, what an item costs or grants while merely held, or a bare `???`.
- **Effects** - every status change, whether it lands on you or on everyone nearby. The
  distinction is carried by the item, not the layout; no item states both.
- **Cooking** - what one more turn on the fire does.
- **Weight** - always last, flush under Cooking, so the eye learns where to find it. Those two
  are the lines about the item rather than its effects, and reading them as one group is what
  stops the overlay looking like it has a stray trailing line.

This replaced a five-block model (Note, Status, Others, State, Weight). State only ever held
the uses label, which was dropped; Status and Others merged because across every item only
one had content in both.

**Passives go in Custom, not Effects.** The Ancient Idol's `∞ {shield}` and the Cactus's
`+20 {thorns}` are status changes, but Effects answers "what happens when you use this", and
neither item is ever used - filing them there would promise an action that does not exist.
There is no "while held" marker and none is needed: an item with a passive has no use-action
to be confused with. Both are described only because an existing form already fitted them (the
healing amulet already prints `4s {shield}`; thorns already have a token). The Cursed Skull's
`???` leads Custom above everything the item gives everyone else, because "the worst thing
happens to you" is the whole message.

## Reading a line

**The sign says which way a status moves.** `+30 {hot}`, `-20 {hunger}`. The colour is the
status's own, taken from the game's bars, not whether the change is good for you: the icon
already carries that meaning, and tying colour to the status keeps it consistent with the HUD.
An amount that belongs to no single status - a shared budget, an item glyph - wears the HUD's
cream.

**Over time, in one of three forms**, and the choice is made in one place so two items never
state the same kind of effect differently:

- `-35 {injury} / 15s` - **total and duration**, the default. It answers "what does this do to
  me", and a duration you can wait out.
- `-6 {cold} /s → 360s` - **rate and duration**, where the total runs past a full bar and so
  describes arithmetic rather than anything a player feels. A Heat Pack's 6 a second across
  360 seconds is 2160 on a scale that stops at 100.
- `-5 {cold} /s` - **rate alone**, for the one case with no duration to state: a lantern warms
  you until its fuel runs out, and a total would make a nearly-spent one read differently from
  a full one.

**A range means "this varies when you use it".** The Scorpion's `50-105 {poison} / 20s` and the
healing amulet's `+7-15 {petrify}` both depend on your state at the moment of use. The
Shroomberry's `+0-15 {stamina}` is the one exception, and a deliberate one: the value is fixed
at level generation and readable, and it is withheld because the berry is a gamble, exactly as
`????` withholds the effect. A range is never used to paper over a value that could be read;
that is what makes the Scorpion's worth trusting.

**A slash means one thing in each position.** Between *icons* it is one amount shared across
those statuses - `-60 {injury}/{poison}/{cold}...`, Scout's Tenacity's budget, sixty points of
relief split between them rather than sixty off each - where a space would mean the amount
applies to each. Between *numbers* it is a discrete pair, one of which happens: the duplication
amulet's `+7/15 {petrify}` for plain against mystical, and `+ 0/20 {poison} / 8s` for a
mushroom the overlay has been told not to tell apart from its poisonous twin, where the sign
leads on its own because it belongs to both outcomes.

**An arrow means "and then".** An effect that lands when a timer ends is not a line of its own;
it is welded onto the line that starts the timer: Energy Drink's `8s {stamina}{stamina}{stamina}
→ +25 {drowsy}` is one statement, faster now and sleepy afterwards. The same arrow says "becomes"
- `{item} → {item}{item}` for the duplication amulet, `{cook} → 4.8m` for an item that cooks
into a blast - and "reshuffled" for the Chaos berry's `-100 {poison} → +? {poison}`, one line
per status so the randomisation reads as one shuffle rather than two unrelated effects.

**`∞` is infinite.** `8s ∞ {stamina}` for infinite stamina, the mark spaced away from the icon
because flush it read as one glyph. The Ancient Idol's `∞ {shield}` and the Magic Bugle's
`∞ {stamina} 12m` are the same mark as the figure. Three stamina icons in a row mean *faster*
(Energy Drink, Scout's Ambition's run boost) and deliberately not infinity: the stamina is not
infinite there, only the movement is quicker.

**A balloon means you float.** Scout's Initiative drops gravity rather than granting speed, and
a balloon is the game's own picture of that; the bunch is drawn for three balloons' worth or
more, the game's own second picture of the same lift.

**A number and its unit are one token**: `8s`, `4.8m`, no space. Distances are in the metres the
altitude readout shows unless config says Unity units. An area effect's reach trails its
figure on the same line - `+27.5 {injury} 19m` - the same shape everywhere, and what tells
dynamite's blast line from its held-injury line at a glance.

**Icons run three to a line.** Past that the eye stops counting and the box wraps at an
arbitrary point; three rather than four because Tenacity heals six statuses and four broke
that into an uneven 4 and 2.

**One line per status.** A clear-all is one `-100 {x}` per status it removes, never a pooled
`-100 {seven icons}` - that reads as a shared budget, which is emphatically not what clearing
everything is. Leaving the excluded statuses out of the run says which are spared without
naming them. A lantern's field is one line per status too; grouping by shared rate was tried
and dropped so a reader never learns a second way of reading a line.

**Sunscreen is a bare duration.** Two icons were tried and dropped: heat behind a shield
overpromises, because the protection is from the *sun* and a campfire will still cook you; a
parasol is accurate but reads as a different item. The item's own icon on the HUD already says
what it is.

**`????` is a Shroomberry's effect**, coloured green or red by whether this berry's slot is
guaranteed good or bad, half-and-half where it is a genuine coin flip. `???` on the Cursed
Skull is "the worst thing".

## Ordering effects

No branch decides where its line goes. A branch says what it means - when the change lands,
which status it moves, which way - and one comparer places it. Three keys in turn:

1. **Onset.** Instant before over-time. Energy Drink strips 100 Drowsy on drinking and hands
   25 back when the boost ends; the other order says the opposite of what happens.
2. **Status**, by a curated order. Hunger and Extra Stamina lead because they are the two
   figures a player looks for first. Poison sits beside Spores and Cold beside Hot because
   curing one so often touches the other. Curse trails because it is a cost rather than an
   effect.
3. **Source component**, which between two changes to one status is the order the game applies
   them - and with a status clamped at zero, the order changes the answer. The Book of Bones
   carries `Curse +50` then `Curse -25` and nets +25 from any starting point; sorted the other
   way it read as -25 then +50, which to anyone with no curse implies +50.

**Petrify sorts last, under everything, whatever its onset.** It is a price rather than an
effect - the amulets charge it for what they just did - so it reads as a footnote to the lines
above. This is the single override of the keys; without it the healing amulet's petrify cost
sorted above the shield it buys.

There used to be a fourth key, removals before additions, and it was a guess at what the
component order actually is. It had been written for Napberry - wipe your drowsiness, then
make you drowsy - and Napberry's clear-all sits before its drowsy addition anyway. Redundant
where it was right and wrong where it was not; prefer reading the order to guessing it.

**A clear-all line is dropped where something else already removes that status.** Napberry
restores 100 hunger *and* clears all status, which was two lines both reading `-100 {hunger}`.
Only a clear is ever dropped, and only against another removal: two ordinary removals stack
and both belong, and a clear standing against an *addition* is the Napberry drowsy case,
which is kept on purpose.

## What is deliberately not shown

**Because there is no symbol for it yet:**

- The uses count.
- `Action_WarpToBiome` - it teleports you to wherever the thrown fungus lands, so the old
  `WARP TO <SEGMENT>` was wrong as well as wordy.
- `Action_SacrificeFriend` - it kills whoever the dagger is *fed to*, never the holder. `???`
  means "the worst thing happens to you", so it said the wrong thing here.
- `Action_BecomeSkeleton` - being a skeleton has no icon and is not a status. It is still felt
  in the lines around it: the Book of Bones' gated curse renders for a human because this
  toggles first.
- The Cactus's throw-charge threshold.
- Passives with nothing to draw: Parasol, Balloon and its bunch, Glider, Jetpack, Backpack
  slot counts (the HUD has no slot icon to scrape, so a bare number would say nothing).

**Because it would mislead:**

- A change gated on being a skeleton is shown plainly when it would land on the observed
  character and omitted otherwise. Hedging it as `0/+50` was tried and read as a coin flip for
  something that is not one. The showcase assumes a human.
- Sunscreen's cloud lifetime (four seconds, beside the ninety the protection lasts): two
  durations on one item read as a puzzle. How long you are covered is what a player acts on.
- The Cursed Skull's 900-unit radius, which means "everyone". The Magic Bugle's half-second
  affliction, which is re-applied every tenth of a second and so is infinite while the horn
  sounds; its reach is shown instead.
- The Scorpion's instant 2.5 poison, folded into the range rather than a line of its own.
- A thrown item's shatter effect on an item that is for drinking: an Antidote breaks into its
  cloud too, and listing the cloud beside the drink read as the same cure twice at two
  strengths. Only an item with no consume action and no uses has nothing else to do but be
  thrown.

**Because it is the player's to find out:**

- What a cooked item becomes (Frog into Frog Legs): a plain `+` rather than the result.
- The peel a Berrynana leaves behind. The peel icons are packed regardless, so this is a
  choice about what to say, not what is reachable.
- The items a breakable turns into (a Coconut's halves, a nest's egg). Only the non-item
  spawns, where an effect lives, are described.
- A Shroomberry's dealt stamina and, for the coin-flip berries, its rolled valence. Both are
  behind spoiler settings that default off.

**Because it is not worth a line:**

- Web, FlyTrap and Thorns on a clear-all. They come from hazards a player meets rarely, so on
  almost every clear-all item they would be lines describing something the reader is not
  carrying. Thorns is already refused by the game; it stays named because the reason is ours
  and would outlive a patch that made thorns curable.
- A second weight figure from a sticky item; no item sets it.
- Dynamite's cooking explosion repeating the blast the Effects section already shows. A rule
  to hide that was written, needed two component-order exceptions inside a week, and was
  stripped as more trouble than one line.

**Because it is dead code:** Mushroom Glow, the climbing-chalk affliction, the healing
amulet's gradual pocket healing, the zombie and campfire afflictions that never reach an item.

## Colours

Status colours are the game's own bar colours, sampled from the HUD at runtime with a table
as fallback, so the overlay always matches the bars. Durations, reaches, arrows and unit
suffixes are a neutral grey. Figures that belong to no status wear the HUD's cream rather than
pure white, so they look like part of the HUD rather than shouting over it; the same cream is
the overlay's base colour, so a missed colour tag degrades quietly instead of rendering
TextMeshPro's white. The cooking hint's `+` and `-` and the Shroomberry's `????` carry their
whole meaning in colour with no number to lean on, so their green and red are saturated rather
than pastel.

Three colours are hand-picked because nothing in the game carries them: numbness, taken from
the icon's pale stems rather than its dark caps; the generic item glyph; the balloon.

**Curse is nearly black, and that is settled.** `#1B0043` is the game's own curse colour, it is
legible enough against the overlay in play, and it reads as cursed in a way no brighter
substitute would. It has been raised as a legibility problem more than once and it is not one.

Only silhouettes are tinted. The game's status icons are white shapes coloured by the HUD, so
multiplying them by the text colour reproduces what the HUD shows; artwork with its own
colours - the numbness mushrooms, item icons - is left alone, because tinting it darkens it.

## The cooking hint

One line answering one question: **should this go on the fire again?** It is about the *next*
cook, not the cooking already done, and getting that wrong is what made a Big Lollipop already
at stage 1 promise a gain for a second cook that changes nothing.

| Line | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `+ {cook}` | the next cook improves it |
| `+++ {cook}` | the one gain nothing else offers: a cook that makes an item cure curse |
| `- {cook}` | the next cook makes it worse - burnt, poisoned |
| `--- {cook}` | the next cook destroys it - wrecked, popped, melted |
| `{cook} → 4.8m [+17.5 {injury}]` | cooking turns it into a blast over that radius |
| nothing | cooking is impossible, or the next cook changes nothing worth saying |

The blast form uses the campfire, not a dynamite glyph: dynamite reads as danger, but most of
these explosions help - Antidote, Cure-All and the Faerie Lantern all become a cloud of what
they do. Where an explosion hurts, the injury figure says so outright, and it is the figure
you would actually receive standing where you are, not the prefab's advertised amount. "It
becomes a cloud of what it does" is the whole message; the cloud's own effect is not repeated
on the line.

Behaviours are judged by what kind they are, never by which item carries them. Reaching stage
2 says nothing, because it changes nothing; saying `+` there told players to cook a Big
Lollipop from Cooked to Well Done for a benefit that does not exist. Cooking changes the item's
other numbers in place, so the rest of the overlay is already right without help from this
line.

## Settings

Everything defaults on except the spoilers. The mod's whole purpose is the information, so a
player who wants less of it switches sections off rather than the reverse.

- **Sections** - one toggle each, in the order the overlay draws them.
- **Appearance** - font size is the single knob for how big everything is; icons scale with it.
  Outline, line spacing, icon sharpness (the game's icons have very soft edges, which read as
  blurry at overlay size; 1 leaves them as the game has them), a blank line between sections,
  box width, and offsets measured from the inventory slot so the overlay follows the selection
  at any resolution.
- **Status Scale** - what a full bar reads as. At 100 a Granola Bar restores 15; at 40 it reads
  6. Decimals follow the scale so the game's step is never rounded away.
- **Show Unity Meters** - raw units instead of the altitude readout's metres, for players who
  think in what every other measuring mod reports.
- **Shroomberry Effect Hint** (on) - colours `????` by whether the berry is good or bad. Red
  and yellow berries are always good and green and blue always bad, which anyone can remember
  after a run or two, so it spoils nothing.
- **Spoil Purple Shroomberry** (off) - the purple berry can roll either way; on, the overlay
  reads this map's roll and commits to one colour. Shows information the game hides.
- **Spoil Shroomberry Energy Increase** (off) - the dealt stamina instead of the 0-15 span.
  Same reason.
- **Do Not Distinguish Poisonous Mushrooms** (off) - some mushrooms have a poisonous twin that
  looks slightly different and carries the same name. Off, the poisonous one shows its poison
  and the safe one shows none. On, both show `+ 0/20 {poison} / 8s`, for players who enjoy
  telling the twins apart by eye.
- **Show Real Rope Length** (off) - by default the Rope Cannon shows its rope in the same
  figure the Rope Spool's own UI uses (7.5m), so the two never disagree in front of a player.
  On, it shows the derived span, consistent with every other distance but breaking parity with
  the spool.
- **Periodic Refreshes** (on, advanced) - re-check once a second, which is what catches
  another player swapping the item in their hands while you watch them.
- **Debug Logging** (off, advanced) - the diagnostics in
  [internals_infra.md](internals_infra.md#diagnostics).
